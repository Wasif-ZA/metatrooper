Pipeline map: what you run, what the market runs

2026-09-29T11:40+10:00. Built from 346 of your AI sessions on laptop-ops, plus two separate market scans (web search, and Agent-Reach).

1. The answer

Yes, an IDE built around your pipelines is possible. About two thirds of your work runs one loop: spec, build, Codex review, Gemini review, reconcile, check it visually, hand back.

That loop is coding-shaped. The coding IDE market is crowded, with 20+ tools. So build it for yourself first, and do not sell it as a coding IDE.

Both market scans picked the same top 2 for selling: (1) social media plus video repurposing, and (2) SEO. Demand exists, and connectors (MCP servers) already exist. What nobody has built is a workspace to preview, approve, schedule and replay the work.

Your own history already touches #1. You have client prospecting for video-editing clients, and the OpusClip MCP is installed. So the path is: your coding loop as the base, then one social-and-video workspace on top.

What each engine does for you

  • Claude is the orchestrator. In the 53 full transcripts it made 4,227 tool calls: 2,838 Bash, 410 Edit, 314 Write, 238 Read, and 86 multiple-choice questions to you.
  • Codex is your reviewer. Of its 41 sessions outside ACU and smoke tests, 23 were read-only reviews. Another 6 were Astra art direction. Only 2 were builds.
  • Gemini is a reviewer and bulk worker. Of its 35 real runs, 19 were reviews and 11 applied edits you had already decided. 72 of its 73 runs were one prompt with no follow-up.
  • They run as a pair. Codex handed off to Gemini 5 times and Gemini to Codex 4 times. On 7 of Gemini's 8 active days, Codex also ran.

Where your time leaks

  1. Visual redo loops. There were 36 corrections like "worse now" and "wrong repo" (30 in ACU, 6 in 49 frontend prompts). There is no before/after view, so every comparison is built by hand.
  2. Engine plumbing. 26 of 183 engine runs (14%) only checked that the engine still worked. There were 22 plugin, MCP and login commands. Codex quota ran out and forced a switch to Gemini.
  3. Losing your place. There were 38 /clear, 12 /resume, 22 "continue" re-asks, and 24 "what are you doing / how long left" asks. 58% of your prompts were sent while another session was still running.

Where they agree

  • Top 2: social/video and SEO. In both, the gap is a workspace (preview, approve, schedule, run history), not the capability. MCPs for Buffer, OpusClip, Ahrefs and Semrush already exist.
  • Skip these: computer use and design (the labs and Figma own them), and inbox/calendar (Google, Microsoft and Anthropic own it).
  • Coding is crowded. Superset, Conductor, the Codex app, Claude Code desktop, Cursor 3 and 20+ open-source runners are already there.

Where they disagree

  • Lead gen: web search ranks it #4. Agent-Reach drops it because of deliverability, LinkedIn bans and spam law.
  • Browser QA: web search ranks it #3. Agent-Reach treats it as plumbing that belongs inside the other workspaces.

Top unmet needs (from real complaints, Agent-Reach)

  1. A stop before anything external happens. One HN thread (79 comments) describes Claude Code signing a contract without asking.
  2. A way to see what the agent actually did. In one case the logs looked clean while a captcha loop left 3 of 30 rows filled.
  3. Memory per project or client, so you stop re-explaining context every day.
  4. Windows and Linux support, no forced login, and bring-your-own subscription. These top the issue lists for OpenClaw (95 reactions), Superset and AionUi.
  5. Spend caps and a live cost meter. There's an HN story about a $78k Codex runaway.

5. The IDE shape these numbers point to

Version 0: for you only

Each pane below replaces something you currently do by hand.

  1. Agent board. One card per running session or engine, showing what it's doing, how far along it is, and which engine it uses. Replaces the 24 status asks and 22 "continue" re-asks.
  2. Engine lights. Always-visible Claude / Codex / Gemini health and quota. Replaces the 26 smoke-test runs and the 22 plugin and login commands.
  3. Review lane. One button runs Codex review and Gemini review on the same diff and shows what both found, what only one found, and where they disagree. Replaces the 9 manual handoffs and matches your CLAUDE.md audit rule.
  4. Before/after pane. Screenshots at 390px and 1280px, with a hold-to-swap view. Replaces the 12 pasted screenshots, 29 "show me" asks and the redo loops.
  5. Hand-back tray. Staged diff, drafted commit message, and the exact command for you to run. The IDE never commits.

Version 1: the part other people might pay for

A social-and-video workspace: long video in, a week of approved posts out. It uses OpusClip for clips and Buffer or Postiz for posting, with a preview grid and an approval queue before anything goes live.

Build on, not from scratch

AionUi (33.2k stars) already detects Claude Code, Codex and 20+ CLIs, and it has cron. Superset (14.7k stars) has worktrees and an in-app browser. Check whether either can host your panes before writing a shell of your own.

6. Risks

The two scans contradict each other on Anthropic billing. Agent-Reach says that since 2026-06-15, headless Claude Code (claude -p), the Agent SDK and third-party tools bill against a separate monthly credit at API rates. Web search says a May billing change for tools like Conductor was "delayed indefinitely" in June. Neither was checked against Anthropic's own pages. Both scans give the same hedge: run the normal interactive claude in a terminal pane, the way Superset and Conductor do, and treat Codex and Gemini as equal engines.
  1. The labs are shipping this themselves. Examples: Cowork (merged into Claude chat 2026-09-16), the Claude Code desktop parallel sessions, the Codex app and ChatGPT Work. Only depth in one pipeline is safe: its data, its preview and its approval rules.
  2. Distribution, not building, is your known bottleneck. Your own notes say the usual cold-start income is A$0. Version 1 only makes money if the video clients from your prospecting lanes use it.
  3. Source quality. Many prices come from aggregator sites, and the market sizes differ between analyst firms. The GitHub stars and YouTube view counts are live and exact.

7. How this was made

  • Your history: 163 Claude Code sessions (845 prompts, 2026-06-01 to 2026-09-29), 53 full transcripts, 110 Codex sessions, 73 Gemini conversations, 473 git commits and 219 activity-log entries. Everything came from this laptop, so the Dell and the main PC are not included.
  • Privacy: any session that touched ACU was counted and categorised only. None of its wording was read into this report.
  • Lanes: tool and skill counts came from a deterministic node script. A subagent sorted the prompts into categories. One market scan used web search. The other used Agent-Reach: GitHub CLI, Exa, YouTube through yt-dlp, the HN Algolia API and r.jina.ai.
